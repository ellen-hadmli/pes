## Текст страницы

🚨 LONG RANT ALERT 🚨
There’s a phrase that keeps making the rounds in Hekatean spaces, repeated like some piece of ancient wisdom by people who should really know better: “Hekate works from afar.” You’ll find that shit all over the place.. in introductory witchcraft books, in forum posts, in the breathless UPG confessions of people who’ve been at this for six months and apparently decided that was long enough to start publishing theological positions. Sometimes it gets fancied up in occult language: magick itself is action at a distance, you see. As if slapping a philosophical gloss on sloppy scholarship makes it less sloppy. People have even tried to spin the “from afar” bullshit into a spiritual concept, claiming it captures something true about how She operates, influencing events from the cosmic green room, apparently never quite making it to the stage.

Today this idea gets dragged out behind the barn, shot, and buried once and for all.

The “from afar” claim traces back to a Greek word: hekas, meaning “far off.” The argument goes that Hekate’s name derives from this root, and therefore Her very name encodes the concept of distance. Some academics have also suggested Her name is simply the feminine form of Apollo’s epithet Hekatos, “far-darting” or “worker from afar,” as if Hekate is just a gender-swapped Apollo with longer hair and a fantabulous key collection. The academic kill shot, though, is this: Her name is probably not even Greek!

The most rigorous modern scholarship doesn’t support a clean Greek etymology for “Hekate” at all. Linguist R.S.P. Beekes, one of the most respected figures in Greek historical linguistics, outright rejected the Greek origin of Her name and argued instead for a Pre-Greek, Anatolian root. That totally tracks, because Her oldest known worship wasn’t in Greece. It was in Caria, in what is now southwestern Turkey. The Karians used the root of Her name natively, in their own language, centuries before She was absorbed into the Greek pantheon. There’s a 4th-century BCE Karian dynast named Hekatomnos, and those linguistic roots are sitting right there in the dirt of Asia Minor, waiting for anyone who actually bothers to look before they start writing books. Trying to define a primordial Titanic force using a back-translated Greek word is like trying to measure the ocean with a fucking ruler.

And even if you want to stay within Greek territory, there’s a far more compelling etymological candidate that the “from afar” crowd consistently ignores. Prominent scholars, including Sarah Iles Johnston, have pointed to the Greek ekati or hekōn as a legitimate root, and that doesn’t mean “far off.” It means by whose will. Willing. She who works Her will. A Sovereign Queen whose will is the operative force in the equation. It’s funny how that never makes it into the introductory witchcraft books, isn’t it?

In Hesiod’s Theogony, the passage dedicated to Hekate reads almost like a litany, and every single blessing attributed to Her circles back to the same thing: Her willingness to grant it. Kings derive their authority through Her, athletes win through Her, warriors triumph through Her. She stands by horsemen “whom She will,” and of those who work the sea and pray to Her, the text says She “gives great catch, and easily She takes it away as soon as seen, if so She will.” And then there’s the wealth line, which is my personal favorite: “For to this day, whenever any one of men on earth offers rich sacrifices and prays for favor according to custom, he calls upon Hekate. Great honor comes full easily to him whose prayers the Goddess receives favorably, and She bestows wealth upon him; for the power surely is with Her.” The power is with Her. Not broadcast from afar, not chucked across some vast celestial gulf, but with Her, and that power is Her will, the same ethelō threading through every single line of that passage. Ethelō: volition, willingness, the active exercise of divine will, the mechanism by which every blessing in that entire passage flows. The prayers are received because She is present. The wealth is bestowed because She wills it. Hesiod was kind enough to write every bit of that down for us roughly 2,700 years ago. Thanks, Hesiod. Real bro move. So there’s really no excuse for this “from afar” bullshit.

The Chaldean Oracles push this even further, and honestly if the Hesiod passage didn’t already make you feel stupid for ever entertaining the “from afar” bullshit, this one is going to finish the job. In that cosmological framework, Hekate is the World Soul, the Anima Mundi, the cosmic intermediary through which the Father’s fire, the primordial creative force of existence itself, descends into material reality. She is the iynx, a spinning, magnetic, generative force whose entire nature is built around divine will as the mechanism that pulls reality into form. Her desire, Her will, is what animates matter itself, which means that every single thing you can see, touch, taste, or measure exists because She willed it into being. Every. Single. Thing. She didn’t set creation in motion and then fuck off to let it run itself. She is the animating principle at the center of all of it, the hinge on which existence itself turns, and She has been there since before the concept of “distance” even existed. She holds the keys to the empyrean realms above and the underworld below, and Her dominion over earth, sky, and sea isn’t a title or a trophy or some bureaucratic portfolio. It is what She is. So the next time someone tells you She works from afar, feel free to hand them a copy of the Chaldean Oracles and wish them well. And btw, She has been pushing me toward this view of Her for a while now through my own practice and gnosis, so take that for whatever it’s worth.

So for anyone who apparently slept through all of that: She is the World Soul. Her will is a cosmological mechanism. She is woven into the fabric of reality itself, the torchlight at the crossroads and the crossroads themselves. “Worker from afar”? Give me a fucking break.

She is not somewhere else nudging and poking shit with a long stick. Invoking Her is not sending a message across a vast celestial distance and hoping She gets it, because She is already there and She has always been there. The question has never been whether She’s present. The question is whether *you* are.

Which brings me to the other thing I need to address, because it pisses me off just as much and it’s cut from the same cloth.

There’s a story that I see in Hekatean spaces so often it has its own gravitational pull at this point. Someone practices for a while, feels the connection, does the work, and then reports that Hekate has “pulled back,” gone distant, stopped communicating. And people nod along sympathetically, like yes, that’s just what She does sometimes, She works in mysterious ways, She recedes.

Bullshit.

If you feel like Hekate has gone quiet, I’m just going to say what everyone else is way too polite to say: you fucked up the relationship and you’re blaming Her for it. You got lazy, you got inconsistent, you started treating your practice like a hobby you pick up when the mood strikes, and now you have the nerve to sit in your little witch forums and muse about how Hekate feels distant lately like She’s the problem. She’s not the problem. You are. You stopped showing up. You stopped putting in the work. You started coasting on early experiences and calling it devotion, and at some point the signal got so weak on your end that you convinced yourself the tower went down. It didn’t because it never does on Her end. She is the World Soul. Her will is the animating force of material existence itself, woven into every atom of every thing that has ever existed, and you think She pulled back from YOU? Get the fuck out of here with that.

Part of what fuels this narrative is the way a lot of people approach devotion in the first place, and I’m going to be blunt about it because the witchcraft community has been coddling this particular brand of laziness for way too long. There’s a model out there that treats Hekate like a utility: leave an offering at the crossroads during the Deipnon, light a candle when you need something, do the bare minimum of acknowledgment, and check back in next month when you need something else. She gets summoned when wanted and otherwise left completely alone, like some divine vending machine you shake when nothing comes out. You want Her to walk with you through your life but you can’t be bothered to show up for Her unless you need your rent paid or your ex cursed. Spiritual freeloading dressed up in a black robe, and the fact that it’s normalized in so many witchcraft spaces is genuinely embarrassing. You cannot build genuine connection with anyone, human or divine, on a schedule of once-monthly minimal contact and a plate of food. How the hell can you expect a Goddess to walk with you when you’re only sending out a “hey, hope you’re doing well” text once a month and then losing your mind when the line feels dead?

And then there are the practitioners who slot Her into a roster like She’s a substitute teacher, Hekate as one of several deities given equal billing, a side dish at the pantheon buffet, and honestly the audacity of that still gets me every single time. Look, this isn’t a dig at anyone else’s gods, because that’s not what this is about and frankly it’s beneath the point. What this is about is the jaw-dropping nerve it takes to call yourself a devotee of Hekate while treating Her like She’s interchangeable with whoever else is on your altar that week. She is the World Soul. The Queen of Witches. The Mother of all. The animating force of material existence. And you’ve got Her sandwiched between a crystal and a printout of some other deity’s sigil like She’s lucky to be included. If you want a real relationship with Her, and I mean a real one, not the thin performative version that makes you feel spiritual without actually requiring anything of you, then you need to decide what you actually want and commit to it fully. She doesn’t do half measures. And if the relationship feels empty and distant and transactional, you already know why. You built it that way. Own it.

The “from afar” interpretation is bad scholarship, and beyond the etymology it creates a theological framework where distance gets baked into the relationship before it even starts, which is arguably even more damaging than the bad etymology itself. It hands people a ready-made excuse for why the connection feels weak, one that conveniently places the reason everywhere except where it actually belongs. It lets the lazy practitioner off the hook. It gives the inconsistent devotee something to point at besides their own reflection. It reframes personal failure as divine mystery and calls it wisdom, and that might be the most insidious thing about it, because it sounds profound enough that people accept it without question and pass it along to the next person who comes looking for answers. It is an excuse wearing the costume of esoteric insight, and it does a genuine disservice to every serious devotee who has actually done the work, cracked the texts, shown up consistently, and built a real relationship with Her based on who and what She actually is.

Hekate is not distant. She is the fire that descends from the empyrean and the darkness that rises from beneath the earth and the crossroads where those two forces meet, and She holds the keys to all of it. Her will is not transmitted across some vast cosmic distance, because Her will is the cosmos, the animating force at the center of everything that has ever existed or ever will. To call Her a worker from afar isn’t just bad scholarship, it’s an embarrassing failure of imagination from people who clearly never bothered to look past the first Google result. This is the World Soul we are talking about. The Queen of Witches. The Mother of all. The force whose desire literally pulls matter into form. She doesn’t work from afar any more than gravity does, and if you’ve been treating Her like some distant cosmic middle manager who occasionally glances down to check Her messages, it is long past time to rethink your entire approach. Bury the concept and do it with purpose. And then go to your altar and act like you know who the fuck you’re dealing with.
