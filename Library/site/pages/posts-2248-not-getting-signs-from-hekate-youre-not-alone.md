## Текст страницы

In a previous blog, I talked about the tendency people have to overanalyze every weird moment and immediately label it a “sign from Hekate,” as if a Goddess of liminality needs to communicate through random noise because discernment is apparently out of fashion. That post focused on not assigning divine meaning to every dog bark or half-remembered dream, but there’s another side of this same obsession that shows up just as often and tends to mess people up even harder. What if you’re not seeing anything at all? No signs, no dreams, no dramatic animal encounters, and no spooky midnight confirmation that you’re special, chosen, or somehow on a cosmic short list.

Instead, what you’re left with is curiosity mixed with a persistent pull toward Hekate, even though nothing flashy or theatrical seems to be happening.

That’s usually where the anxiety kicks in, especially after people spend too much time soaking in social media where everyone else appears to have a cinematic origin story complete with ominous music and comment sections full of validation. Someone dreamed Hekate appeared and spoke to them directly. Someone else swears dogs suddenly follow them everywhere. Someone claims a black dog stared into their soul like it was about to hand them a sacred quest. The stories pile up, the aesthetics get darker, the language gets grander, and suddenly you’re left wondering why none of that is happening to you and whether that means you’ve been ignored, rejected, or deemed unworthy by a Goddess.

Here’s the part that people don’t like hearing because it punctures the fantasy really fuckin’ quick. A lot of what you’re seeing online is exaggeration, projection, and people getting high on the idea of being significant. Not always because they’re lying outright, but because attention feels good and spiritual spaces reward big stories. When someone is actively hunting for signs, everything starts glowing with importance. Dreams shaped by recent reading turn into messages. Coincidences become confirmations. Vibes get promoted to revelations. None of that automatically means Hekate has nothing to do with it, but it sure as hell doesn’t mean those experiences are the standard you should be measuring yourself against. More importantly, the absence of theatrics does not mean you’re doing something wrong.

One of the most deeply ingrained assumptions people carry, usually without ever examining it, is that a real relationship with Hekate would announce itself loudly and dramatically, like a goddamn movie trailer. As if a Goddess of thresholds, in-between spaces, and quiet reckonings would need to bang pots and pans together to get someone’s attention. That expectation has nothing to do with Hekate and everything to do with insecurity, comparison, and the very human need to be reassured that you matter in some cosmic, externally validated way.

There’s an even uglier layer underneath that assumption. The belief that Hekate should have to initiate contact in order for your interest to be legitimate quietly turns Her into a deity who recruits followers, hands out invitations, and singles people out like they won a spiritual lottery. That framing might feel flattering, but it doesn’t line up with history and it doesn’t line up with how long-term practitioners actually experience Her. When you sit with it honestly, it starts to look a lot like ego wearing ritual clothing.

If you’re waiting to be chosen, you’re already centering yourself in a way that should make you uncomfortable.

What actually tends to happen, when this is real, is far less sexy and far more grounded. People become interested in Hekate when they hit a liminal point in their own lives, when something ends, breaks down, or stops working and they can’t bullshit their way through it anymore. At that point, the in-between becomes unavoidable. Her domain suddenly makes sense, not because She decided to notice you, but because you finally noticed where the fuck you’re standing. That pull doesn’t need signs to justify it! 

This is where the honesty has to sharpen, because this is the question most people avoid. Why do you want signs so badly? Is it because you genuinely want to engage with Hekate in a way that will challenge you, strip away illusion, and force you to confront uncomfortable truths about yourself? Or is it because She looks cool, dark, and dangerous and makes you feel edgy, powerful, or different from everyone else? Are you interested in the work itself, or are you interested in the aesthetic and the story you get to tell about yourself?

Those motivations are not the same thing, and Hekate does not exist to prop up your ego.

If your interest is rooted in looking cool, feeling chosen, or having a dramatic origin story you can point to, then the lack of signs is going to feel like rejection. If your interest is rooted in curiosity, seriousness, and a willingness to stand in uncertainty without being constantly reassured, then the absence of spectacle won’t bother you nearly as much.

You don’t need a vision, an omen, a haunted crow, or a black dog staring into your soul to justify paying attention. You also don’t need to believe that a Goddess is hovering nearby watching you and waiting for you to notice Her, because that narrative says far more about human insecurity than divine reality.

If Hekate matters to you, then engage that honestly. Read, reflect, and sit quietly. Pay attention to what actually changes in you over time. Notice whether your thinking sharpens, whether your boundaries firm up, and whether you become more comfortable standing at endings and thresholds without demanding reassurance every five minutes. Notice whether your interest makes you more grounded, or just more invested in spiritual theatrics and performance. That, my friends, is the real test.

So if you’re sitting there freaking out because nothing dramatic has happened, stop pretending that means you’ve been overlooked and start accepting that it means you’re not living inside a fantasy. Hekate is not a fucking theme park attraction, and She is not staging thunder, howling dogs, or spooky omens to make insecure people feel important. The expectation that a Goddess should perform for you on demand is not spirituality, it’s ego masturbation with a mythological costume slapped on it.

And let’s be real about the people constantly claiming nonstop signs, visions, and personal divine attention. A good chunk of them are egomaniacs full of shit who are desperate to feel special, darker, chosen, or elevated above everyone else, and another chunk are genuinely disconnected from reality and being encouraged instead of challenged. That isn’t depth and it sure as hell isn’t devotion. That’s narcissism, delusion, or straight-up lying dressed up as mystical experience, and the internet eats it up because it looks cool and sells bullshit.

Some paths don’t begin with spectacle because spectacle attracts people who crave attention, not people who can handle truth. Recognition doesn’t announce itself to soothe fragile egos, and Hekate does not reward self-importance with personal divine theater. If you need a sign that badly, what you actually need is a fucking reality check, not a Goddess.
